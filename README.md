## Residential proxies vs. ISP proxies: choose based on the job

The terminology is crowded, so it helps to separate the two categories before comparing prices.

### Rotating residential proxies

Rotating residential proxies use a pool of IPs associated with residential internet connections. The IP can change by request, after a time interval, or when you explicitly rotate the session.

They are generally better suited to work where geographic diversity or high-volume distribution matters more than keeping the same identity for a long time. Pricing is often based on traffic consumption, usually by GB.

Use this model when you need permitted, distributed access and do not need to stay on one IP for an extended session.

### Static ISP proxies

Static ISP proxies retain the same IP during the rental period. They tend to make more sense for workflows involving stable sessions, repeat testing, or authorized account operations.

HypeProxies’ public ISP plans use per-IP monthly pricing rather than per-GB billing. That matters if your traffic level is high: a bandwidth allowance can become a budget headache faster than expected, especially if a script retries heavily or downloads more data than planned.

### The practical choice

Choose rotating residential proxies when the requirement is **IP rotation and geographic breadth**.

Choose static ISP proxies when the requirement is **session consistency, fixed IP allocation, and uncapped traffic**.

Do not buy a rotating product merely because it sounds more “residential.” If your job requires the same IP tomorrow that you used today, rotation is the wrong feature.
